LinearSolveBench (also styled LinearSolverBench in the post body) asks a model or harness to write fast, accurate, general numerical solvers for large sparse linear systems in C. That is a meaningful departure from typical coding arenas that reward syntax-valid snippets or unit-test pass rates on toy algorithms. Sparse linear solves sit at the core of scientific computing, FEM, graph Laplacians, and many robotics/control pipelines. Measuring wall-clock quality, numerical accuracy, and generality forces evaluation designers to confront harness realities: compiler flags, BLAS/LAPACK linkage, timeout budgets, matrix families (SPD vs indefinite), and whether the judge accepts approximate iterative methods or demands direct factorization. For infra teams, the practical implication is that “coding LLM” scoreboards that ignore numeric stability and performance will mis-rank systems that look strong on LeetCode-style tasks but collapse on production numerical workloads.
